# Incidents and a transport

Generated by `run_pass4.py` in 18 s. Per-part rows use the real plan, real lead times and real unit costs, with **simulated** stock and open orders.

## 1. Severity per supplier

An incident is one decision with one owner: a supplier that has slipped puts every part it ships at risk at once, and the response is one phone call. One incident per *(supplier, severity)*, because a supplier with two P1 parts and nine P4s is two conversations on two clocks.

### At the simulated stock level

| | alerts | incidents |

| items | 209 | **30** (86% fewer) |
| P1 items | 0 | 0 |
| busiest owner | BUYER 209 | BUYER **30** |
| parts covered | 209 | 209 |
| value at risk | 14,730,015 | 14,730,015 |

82 of 209 parts run out inside 13 weeks and 0 are already past the point where a normal order could arrive in time. **Nothing is P1.** Real lead times are 3-55 days, so every part that will run out can still be ordered in time. The simulated version had 14-75 day lead times and put 26 urgent items on one owner; that finding does not survive real lead times. The grouped queue still fails the load check on total size (30 items for one owner against a limit of 15), but those are routine P3/P4 items that go out as a weekly digest.

## 2. A transport

Exercised on the stress-case incidents (stock x0.25), because at the base stock level there is nothing urgent to send. The receiver is a real HTTP server on a local socket.

80 incidents become **38 messages**: 36 individual P1/P2 items and 2 digests. Routine items go out as one digest per owner and severity.

**At-least-once, and the receiver deduplicates.** The sender marks a message sent only after the POST succeeds, and every message carries an idempotency key.

- Receiver rejects its first 3 requests with a 503. First pass: 2 failures, nothing marked sent.
- Retried on a backoff clock: all 2 messages delivered, receiver saw 5 requests for 2 deliveries and 0 duplicates.
- Re-running the whole job: 38 duplicate enqueues rejected, 0 extra sends, 0 duplicates at the receiver.
- Crash window (delivered, then died before marking): state after crash `{'PENDING': 1}`, re-sent on restart (1), receiver saw 2 requests, recorded 1 delivery and suppressed 1 duplicate.
- Endpoint that never answers: messages land in `DEAD` (`{'DEAD': 2}`), 2 kept in the outbox, none dropped.
- Rate limit of 5 per recipient: 6 sent, 32 deferred, nothing dropped (True), max 5 to one recipient, urgent first.

The generator and the idempotency key are both checked for being identical across separate processes (Python salts string hashing per process, which once made every number in this project differ run to run).
