Manufacturing supply chain · shortage risk

Can the factory build its 13-week plan?

A real power-tool supply chain makes products from purchased parts. A bill of materials (BOM) is the recipe: which parts, and which sub-assemblies, go into each product. We run that BOM, real lead times and a Monte Carlo simulation to answer: how much of the plan can we build, which parts will stop the line, which suppliers can we trust, and who needs to act first.

1 · Can we build the plan?

Buildable units per week, as a range, not one number

Each simulation draws a new lead time for every open order from the part's real lead-time distribution, plus its vendor's real lateness. The band is the middle 80% of outcomes (P10 to P90); the line is the median. The gap to the demand line is the shortfall. Demand is the real average from the data; stock and open orders are simulated.

2 · What is stopping it?

The parts that gate production, and when to order them

Ranked by how often each part was the one that stopped a product in the simulation. The order-by date is the first short week minus the part's P95 lead time (a bad-case delivery) from its real distribution, not the average. Day 0 is today. Vendor = the SCMS vendor mapped to the part (an assumption).
